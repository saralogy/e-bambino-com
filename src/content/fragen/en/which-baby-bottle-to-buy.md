Baby bottles differ mainly in material and nipple, and both matter more than the label on the box. How you feed and clean the bottle matters even more, so this page covers both.

## Is glass or plastic safer for a baby bottle?

Glass is the material that best keeps substances from passing into milk, and German consumer advisers rate it the most suitable choice for that reason. Plastic bottles are lighter and do not shatter, which helps once your baby holds the bottle. Polycarbonate made with BPA is no longer used for baby bottles: it has been banned in the EU since 2011, and in 2012 the FDA stopped allowing it in bottles and sippy cups after makers had already abandoned it. The same type of plastic can behave differently depending on its additives, so the material name alone does not tell you everything.

| Material | Heat and cleaning | Weight and handling | Good to know |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glass | Handles up to 200 °C (392 °F) and hot cleaning | Heavier; can break if dropped | Keeps substances out of milk best |
| Plastic | Check the maker's heat and dishwasher limits | Light and shatterproof; easy for babies to hold | Polycarbonate with BPA no longer used for baby bottles |
| Silicone nipple | Clean after every feed; regular boiling not needed, but sanitize for young babies (see below) | Soft and flexible | Replace if cracked or damaged |
| Rubber (latex) nipple | Clean after every feed; boil now and then | Gets porous over time | Replace regularly |

## Which nipple flow does my baby need?

Pick the flow by how the milk drips, not by the age on the pack. Turn the filled bottle upside down: about one drop per second is right. If milk runs out faster, your baby will gulp and swallow air, while thicker milk may need a slightly larger, medium hole.

## How should I hold the bottle while feeding?

Hold your baby semi-upright and never feed them lying flat, because that raises the risk of choking. Tilt the bottle so the nipple stays full of milk, which keeps swallowed air down, and let your baby pause when they want. Stop when your baby shows they are full, even if the bottle is not empty.

## Is it safe to prop a bottle?

No, never prop a bottle on a pillow, blanket or any other support. A propped bottle raises the risk of choking, ear infections and tooth decay. In 2026 the CPSC warned parents to stop using infant self-feeding pillows right away because of the risk of aspiration and suffocation.

## How do I clean and warm bottles safely?

Clean bottles and nipples after every feeding. Sanitizing matters most for babies under 2 months, babies born prematurely and babies with a weakened immune system. Formula does not need to be warmed, and if you warm a bottle, set it in warm water, never in the microwave, which creates hot spots that can burn your baby's mouth.
