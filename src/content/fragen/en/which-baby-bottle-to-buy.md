Bottles differ mainly in material, teat and how easy they are to clean and use safely. This page compares the material and teat points covered by the sources we checked, and marks the gaps honestly.

## Is glass or plastic the safer material for a baby bottle?
Glass is the material the Verbraucherzentrale rates most suitable for keeping substances out of food, although it is heavier and more fragile than plastic. Polycarbonate has been banned for baby bottles and training cups in the EU since 2011. The Verbraucherzentrale does not recommend specific plastic types, because the same plastic can be harmless or release substances depending on its additives.

## Do I need an anti-colic vent?
The sources we checked do not show whether an anti-colic vent reduces colic or gas, so this page cannot say whether to buy one. The next answer covers teat hole size, which those sources do address. Ask your pediatrician if colic or gas is a concern for your baby.

## Which nipple flow rate fits my baby?
Look for a teat whose hole lets about one drop per second fall from an inverted bottle. A hole that is too large makes babies drink fast and swallow air, while thicker formulas suit a medium hole. The sources we checked do not give flow-rate sizes by age, so use the drop test rather than the age printed on the packaging.

## Is it safe to prop a bottle with a pillow?
No. Never prop a bottle with a pillow, blanket or other support. The CPSC warned in 2026 to stop using infant self-feeding pillows because they can cause aspiration and suffocation. The AAP advises holding the baby semi-upright during bottle feeding and never feeding a baby lying flat, because this raises the choking risk. It also advises keeping the teat full of milk to limit swallowed air.

## How should I clean a baby bottle?
Clean bottles after every feeding, according to the CDC. Sanitizing matters most for babies under 2 months, premature babies and babies with weakened immune systems. German guidance says silicone teats do not need boiling at home, while rubber teats become porous over time and should be replaced or occasionally boiled.
