Starting solids is a gradual step that follows your baby's development, not a single date on the calendar. This page explains the timing windows in the sources we checked and the signs of readiness.

## Is six months the right age to start?
Six months is the usual reference point: WHO-linked guidance says most babies are ready around then, and German guidance gives a wider window. WHO recommends exclusive breastfeeding for about the first six months. Around six months, a baby's energy and nutrient needs exceed what breast milk alone provides, so safe complementary foods should be introduced while breastfeeding continues, up to two years or beyond. German guidance says many babies can learn to eat from a spoon by the start of the fifth month and nearly all by the end of the sixth. For how milk feeding changes at this point, see [how much should a baby drink in the first year](/feeding/how-much-should-baby-drink-first-year/).

## How do I know my baby is ready?
The main readiness signs are that a baby sits upright with support, holds its head steady and has lost the tongue-thrust reflex, according to German guidance. The checklist below lists the signs named in German guidance.

- [ ] Sits upright with support
- [ ] Holds its head steady
- [ ] Has lost the tongue-thrust reflex, so it no longer pushes spoonfuls back out
- [ ] Makes chewing movements
- [ ] Shows interest in what others are eating
- [ ] Reaches for food and puts food or objects into its mouth

## Which first foods work well?
The sources we checked do not name specific first foods, so this page does not recommend any. Your pediatrician can advise on first foods for your baby.

## Should I start with finger food?
Not alone: several specialist bodies advise against starting only with baby-led weaning, because finger food needs a baby to grasp and swallow lumpy food, which some children cannot do at 5 to 7 months.

## When should I talk to a pediatrician before starting?
Talk to your pediatrician before starting if your baby was born early or has a known allergy. The sources we checked do not give specific timing for these cases, so your pediatrician should set the plan. German guidance does not support starting before the completed fourth month.
