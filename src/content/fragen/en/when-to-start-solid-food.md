Starting solids follows your baby's development, not a date on the calendar. Around 6 months, a baby's energy and nutrient needs start to outgrow what breast milk alone provides, so the WHO and AAP both recommend adding solid food then, while breastfeeding or formula continues.

## Is 6 months the right age to start?

Yes, about 6 months is the age the WHO and AAP recommend for most babies. Some babies show the readiness signs a little earlier, from 4 months, and German guidance sets a window from the end of the fourth month to the start of the seventh. Starting before 4 months is not recommended, so if you think your baby is ready early, check with your pediatrician first.

## How do I know my baby is ready?

Your baby is ready when they sit up with little support, hold their head steady and no longer push food straight back out with the tongue. Interest in food is the other big clue. Look for these signs together:

- [ ] Sits upright with little or no support
- [ ] Holds their head up steadily
- [ ] No longer pushes spoonfuls out with the tongue
- [ ] Makes chewing movements
- [ ] Watches you eat and moves their mouth along
- [ ] Reaches for food and brings things to their mouth

## What are good first foods?

Simple, smooth foods such as iron-fortified infant cereal, mashed banana or puréed prunes are common first foods, and no particular order is required. The CDC suggests oat, barley or multigrain cereal rather than only rice cereal. Offer one new food at a time and wait a few days before the next (the CDC suggests 3 to 5), so you can spot any reaction such as a rash, vomiting or diarrhea. Breast milk or formula still provides most of your baby's nutrition in the first year, so solids start as small tastes alongside milk feeds.

## When should my baby try egg, peanut and other allergens?

Once your baby handles a few first foods well, common allergens such as egg, dairy foods like yogurt, wheat, soy, fish and peanut can be added. The AAP says there is no evidence that waiting prevents allergies, and giving peanut early can lower the chance of a peanut allergy. Give peanut as ground peanut or thinned, smooth peanut butter mixed into food, never as whole peanuts, which are a choking hazard for children under 4. If your baby has severe eczema or an egg allergy, talk to your pediatrician before starting peanut.

## What should a baby not eat or drink before age one?

Never give honey before 12 months, because it can cause infant botulism, a rare but serious illness. Cow's milk is not suitable as a main drink before 12 months, although pasteurized plain yogurt without added sugar is fine. Give no juice before 12 months, and keep foods soft and in small pieces to lower the choking risk.

## Can I start with finger food instead of purées?

You can offer soft finger food, but it is best not to rely on it alone at the start. Eating finger food means grasping and swallowing lumpy food, and some babies cannot do this yet at 5 to 7 months, so several specialist bodies advise against starting with finger food alone. Seat your baby upright in a high chair for every meal; see [when a baby can use a high chair](/feeding/high-chair-age/).
