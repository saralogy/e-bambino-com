The short answer: a stroller works from day one, as long as your baby can lie flat in it.

## Why do newborns need to lie flat?

Newborns cannot hold up their heads or keep their airway open in a slumped position. A bassinet or a seat that reclines completely flat supports the whole body. Once your baby can sit with support, usually around six months, an upright seat becomes comfortable and lets them see the world.

## What is a modular stroller or travel system?

One chassis with interchangeable parts: a bassinet for the first months, a seat for later, and often an adapter for an infant car seat. You buy one frame and use it for years. The trade-off is price, and a car seat should not be used for long walks, because babies should not stay in one for hours.

## What should I consider when choosing?

Your daily routes and your home. Rough paths and cobblestones call for larger wheels and good suspension. Stairs, elevators and small car trunks call for a light frame and a compact fold. Measure your trunk and doorways before you buy.

## What safety features matter?

A five-point harness, brakes that are easy to reach and engage, and a canopy for sun and wind. Never hang heavy bags on the handlebar, because the stroller can tip.

| Situation | Good choice |
|---|---|
| Newborn | Bassinet, fully flat seat or carrier |
| About 6 months, sitting with support | Upright stroller seat |
| Rough paths and cobblestones | Larger wheels, suspension |
| Small car or many stairs | Light frame, compact fold |
