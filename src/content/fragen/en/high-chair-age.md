High chairs rarely fail on design and often fail on fit. A chair that is too big or has no footrest makes it hard for a child to sit still and eat.

## From what age is a high chair useful?

From around six months, when your baby can hold their head steady and sit upright with little support. Before that, a high chair has no real use, and propping up a young baby in one is neither comfortable nor safe.

## Which features matter?

A five-point harness that holds shoulders, hips and crotch. A footrest, because dangling legs make sitting unstable and less comfortable. A wide, stable base that does not tip when your child pushes against the table. A tray and seat that are easy to wipe clean. Chairs that adjust in height and depth last longer as your child grows.

## When should my child switch to a regular chair?

When your child can climb in and out safely and sit at the table without the harness, often somewhere between two and three years. A booster seat on a regular chair is a common next step.

## How do I use a high chair safely?

Always use the harness, even for a short meal. Place the chair away from walls, counters and tables your child could push off from. Never leave your child alone in the chair.

| Age | Seating |
|---|---|
| Under 6 months | No high chair yet |
| 6 to 18 months | High chair with five-point harness and footrest |
| 18 months to 3 years | Adjustable high chair or booster seat |
| From about 3 years | Regular chair or booster at the table |
