Electrical accidents at outlets are a typical risk for toddlers, so plan before your child starts exploring. The AAP notes that toddlers most often get shocked when they bite cords or poke metal objects, such as forks or knives, into unprotected outlets or appliances. For cords and other household dangers, see [Dangerous Household Items for Toddlers](/safety/dangerous-household-items-for-toddlers/).

## Which outlet covers can a child not pull out?

CPSC describes the right covers as ones that are hard for a child to remove and large enough that a child cannot choke on them. Test each cover by trying to pull it out yourself. Keep supervising, since determined toddlers have been known to overcome or disable outlet devices.

## From what age should I cover outlets?

CPSC advises covering unused outlets whenever children are present, and our sources do not name a specific starting age. Kindergesundheit-info lists electrical accidents at outlets as typical from about 7 months to 4 years of age.

## Which outlets do parents usually forget?

Outdoor outlets and power strips are the two outlet types Kindergesundheit-info says to secure, with child locks on power strips and protection on outdoor outlets. Keep electrical cords out of reach and sight, and if a child keeps going back to an outlet, block access with furniture. Our sources do not single out floor-level or kitchen outlets, so check every outlet a child can reach.

## Should I replace outlets with tamper-resistant ones?

CPSC recommends tamper-resistant receptacles whenever outlets are replaced. Our sources do not say whether electrical code requires them, so check that before you rely on it.
