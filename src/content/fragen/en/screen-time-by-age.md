Babies and toddlers learn best from real people: talking, playing and reading together. That is why pediatricians recommend holding off on screens in the first years and keeping them small and shared once they start.

## Should babies under 18 months watch screens?

No. The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP), in its age-based guidance for young children ("Media and Young Minds", 2016), advises avoiding screen media for children under 18 months, with video chatting as the one exception. German pediatric guidance goes further: the AWMF guideline and the German parent site Kindergesundheit-info both advise keeping children under 3 away from screens altogether.

## When can a toddler start watching TV or using apps?

From 18 months at the earliest, and only together with you. The AAP says parents who want to introduce digital media at 18 to 24 months should choose high-quality programs or apps and use them with their child, not let the child watch alone. German guidelines are stricter on this point and recommend waiting until age 3, summed up for parents as "screen-free until three."

| Age | AAP 2016 (US) | AWMF guideline (Germany) | What it means at home |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under 18 months | Avoid screens, except video chats | No screens | Video calls with family are fine; keep TV, tablets and phones away |
| 18 to 24 months | Optional: high-quality programs, always watched together | No screens | If you start, choose one good program and watch it with your child |
| 2 to 5 years | Up to 1 hour a day of high-quality programs, ideally watched together | No screens until 3 | Short, shared viewing; talk about what you see |

## How much screen time is OK for a 2- to 5-year-old?

The AAP's limit is no more than 1 hour a day of high-quality programs, ideally watched together so you can talk about what your child is seeing. The AAP's newer 2025 policy statement on digital media moves away from fixed time limits as the main measure and focuses on how media fit into family life and how products are designed. For babies and toddlers, the age-based advice above is still the clearest practical guide.

## Is video chatting with family different from watching TV?

Yes. Video chatting is a two-way conversation with a real person who responds to your child, which is why the AAP makes an exception for it even in the first 18 months. A call with grandparents is a very different thing from a show or an app.

## Why do pediatricians recommend limiting screens so early?

Because young children learn language and social skills through back-and-forth interaction with the people around them, and screens take time away from that. The German AWMF guideline links heavy screen use in toddlers to concerns about language and cognitive development and about self-regulation, while noting that the research is still debated.

## What can I do instead of screens during the day?

Keep some times and places fully screen-free: the AAP recommends screen-free bedrooms, mealtimes and parent-child playtime. Fill the gaps with simple things that need no device, such as talking through everyday tasks, floor play and books. Reading together is one of the easiest habits to start; see [how to read to a baby](/play/how-to-read-to-a-baby/).
