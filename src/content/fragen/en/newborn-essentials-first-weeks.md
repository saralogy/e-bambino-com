Your first weeks at home come with a long list of baby products, and most of it can wait. A safe sleep space for your newborn cannot wait, so start there.

## What sleep setup does a newborn need from day one?
A newborn needs a firm, flat surface, such as a crib, bassinet or play yard, with only a fitted sheet on it. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) calls this its "Bare is Best" rule, because a bare sleep space helps prevent suffocation. Always place your baby on the back to sleep, and move your baby to a crib, bassinet or play yard if he or she falls asleep in a swing, bouncer or similar product.

| Item | Needed on day one | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Crib, bassinet or play yard | Yes | Firm, flat mattress that meets U.S. federal safety standards |
| Fitted sheet | Yes | The only bedding in the sleep space |
| Sleep sack or wearable blanket | Yes | Use instead of loose blankets; it should fit and not cover the head |
| Loose blanket | No | Keep out of the sleep space |
| Pillow | No | Keep out of the sleep space |
| Padded bumper | No | Padded crib bumpers are banned under federal law |

For the crib itself, see [crib bedding for a baby's first year](/nursery/bedding-for-baby-first-year/) and [bassinet, bedside crib or crib](/nursery/bassinet-bedside-crib-or-crib/).

## How many bodysuits, sleep sacks and sheets do I really need?
Official guidance does not give a number for bodysuits, sleep sacks or sheets, so this guide does not recommend one. What matters more is the setup: a fitted sheet on a firm, flat mattress, and a sleep sack or wearable blanket instead of blankets. The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) advises dressing your baby in layers or a wearable blanket, and a sleep sack should fit your baby and not cover the head.

## What do I need for diapering and changing?
No official source we found lists diapering items, so this guide does not give a list. Ask your pediatrician what to stock for diapering and feeding, and keep the sleep space free of anything that is not needed for sleep.

## What can I skip until later?
Nothing that affects sleep should wait. The AAP advises keeping pillows, quilts, comforters, sheepskins, bumper pads and stuffed toys out of the sleep area, and inclined sleep products should not be used at all. For other items, wait until you know what your baby needs.
