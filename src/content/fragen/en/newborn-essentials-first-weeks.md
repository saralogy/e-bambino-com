Baby registries can run to hundreds of items, but the first weeks at home come down to a handful of basics. Get these ready before the due date and buy the rest as you go.

## What sleep setup does a newborn need from day one?

A newborn needs a crib, bassinet or play yard that meets federal safety standards, with a firm, flat mattress and only a fitted sheet. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) sums it up as "Bare is Best": no blankets, pillows, bumpers or soft toys. Always put your baby down on their back, and if your baby falls asleep in a swing or bouncer, move them to their crib or bassinet.

## What should be ready before the baby comes home?

- **Sleep:** a crib, bassinet or play yard with a firm, flat mattress
- **Bedding:** fitted sheets made for that mattress, with a spare for night changes
- **Warmth:** sleep sacks or wearable blankets in your baby's size
- **Clothes:** bodysuits and sleepers in newborn and the next size up
- **Diapering:** diapers in newborn size, wipes and a diaper cream
- **Feeding:** whatever your plan needs, such as bottles if you will bottle-feed
- **Travel:** a rear-facing car seat, installed before the due date

## Do I need a car seat to bring my baby home?

Yes. Your baby rides rear-facing from the very first trip home from the hospital, so install the seat before your due date. A certified child passenger safety technician can check the installation for you. For packing the rest, see the [hospital bag checklist](/checklists/hospital-bag/).

## How many clothes and diapers should I buy?

Start small, because newborns outgrow the first size quickly and every baby is different. Buy a few pieces in newborn size and more in the next size up, and keep receipts until you know what fits. Newborns go through diapers fast, so see [how many diapers a baby uses](/diapering/how-many-diapers-per-day/) to plan your stock.

## What can wait until later?

Most gear beyond sleeping, feeding, diapering and the car seat can wait, including toys, play mats and a high chair. Some things are best skipped altogether: pillows, bumpers, weighted sleep products and inclined sleepers should never be used for sleep. Hot-water bottles and heating pads do not belong in the crib, and powder should stay away from the changing table, because a baby can inhale it.
