European baby sizes follow body length in centimeters, so measuring beats guessing. Brands differ a little, which is why the length matters more than the age printed on the label.

## How do I measure my baby?

Lay your baby on their back, gently straighten the legs and measure from the top of the head to the heel with a soft tape measure. Measure twice and use the larger value. Then pick the size that is equal to or a little above that length.

## When does a newborn fit size 56?

Size 56 is made for babies up to about 56 cm long, which covers most newborns. A diaper takes up room in the leg area, so one-piece sleepers in 56 can feel tight sooner than bodysuits in the same size. Very small or premature babies may need size 50.

## When is size 62 the better choice?

If your baby is longer than about 54 cm at birth, start with 62. It is also the size most babies wear for the longest stretch of the first months, so it is worth buying more of it. When in doubt, choose the bigger size: babies grow into clothes quickly, and slightly loose is more comfortable than tight.

### EU baby sizes at a glance

| EU size | Body length | Typical age | Common label |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | up to 50 cm | premature, first weeks | Preemie |
| 56 | 51 to 56 cm | 0 to 1 month | Newborn |
| 62 | 57 to 62 cm | 1 to 3 months | 0–3 months |
| 68 | 63 to 68 cm | 3 to 6 months | 3–6 months |
| 74 | 69 to 74 cm | 6 to 9 months | 6–9 months |
| 80 | 75 to 80 cm | 9 to 12 months | 9–12 months |

## Why do sizes differ between brands?

Some brands cut small or large, and fabric stretch varies. Check the brand’s size chart, measure your baby, and keep one size ahead in the drawer. Secondhand clothes in size 56 are a good way to cover the short newborn phase cheaply.
