Babies are good at regulating how much they drink, so the amounts below are typical ranges, not targets. Breast milk is the recommended first food: the WHO advises exclusive breastfeeding for about the first six months, then continuing alongside solid food.

## How much formula does a baby drink per feed?

A newborn takes about 1 to 2 oz (30 to 60 mL) per feed in the first days, then about 2 to 3 oz (60 to 90 mL) every 3 to 4 hours. By the end of the first month, most formula-fed babies take 3 to 4 oz (90 to 120 mL) per feed, and by 6 months about 6 to 8 oz (180 to 240 mL) at each of 4 or 5 feeds a day. Most babies do not need more than about 32 oz (950 mL) of formula a day.

| Age | Formula per feed | How often |
|---|---|---|
| First 2 to 3 days | 1 to 2 oz (30 to 60 mL) | Every 2 to 3 hours, 8 to 12 feeds a day |
| First weeks | 2 to 3 oz (60 to 90 mL) | Every 3 to 4 hours |
| End of month 1 | 3 to 4 oz (90 to 120 mL) | Every 3 to 4 hours |
| 6 months | 6 to 8 oz (180 to 240 mL) | 4 or 5 feeds a day, plus solid food |
| 6 to 12 months | Milk stays the main drink | Formula or solids about 5 to 6 times a day |
| Any age | No more than about 32 oz (950 mL) a day | Ask your pediatrician if your baby always wants much more or less |

## How often does a breastfed baby feed?

A breastfed newborn usually nurses about every 2 hours, which adds up to roughly 10 to 12 feeds in 24 hours. Feed when your baby shows hunger cues rather than by the clock, since you cannot see how much milk your baby takes. Nursing fewer than 8 times in 24 hours on most days is a sign to check in with your pediatrician or a lactation consultant.

## How do I know my baby is getting enough?

Steady wet and dirty diapers are the best everyday sign: by 5 to 7 days old, a well-fed newborn has 6 or more wet diapers a day, with pale urine. Babies may lose up to about 8 to 10 percent of their birth weight in the first days, then should start gaining again. Not gaining about 5 to 7 oz a week once the milk comes in is a reason to see your pediatrician. See [how many diapers a baby uses per day](/diapering/how-many-diapers-per-day/) for what to expect.

## Does my baby need water?

Not before 6 months: breast milk or formula covers all of a baby's fluid needs, even in hot weather. From about 6 months you can offer small sips of water, about 4 to 8 oz (0.5 to 1 cup) a day, from a cup. Never add extra water to formula, because it dilutes the nutrients your baby needs and can cause serious health problems.

## How does drinking change after 6 months?

Breast milk or formula stays the main source of nutrition for the whole first year, while solid food is added from around 6 months. Cow's milk is not suitable as a main drink before 12 months, and the CDC and AAP advise no juice at all before 12 months. Once your baby sits well with good head control, they are ready for meals at the table; see [when a baby can use a high chair](/feeding/high-chair-age/).
