Babies do not drink to a fixed schedule, and amounts vary. This page sets out what the sources we checked say about milk and fluids, and where they stop short of giving numbers.

## How much milk does a baby need per day?
In the first four to six months, breast milk or formula provides enough fluid for a baby, according to German guidance. German guidance also says an extra drink of about 200 mL a day is enough on top of that, and package amounts are only a rough guide, because the baby signals hunger and fullness. For formula, most babies do not need more than about 32 oz (950 mL) a day. If a baby always wants more or less than this, ask your pediatrician. If you are formula feeding, see [which formula stage fits each age](/feeding/formula-stage-by-age/).

## How often should a baby feed in the first year?
The sources we checked do not give a verified number of feeds per day for every age, so follow your baby's hunger and fullness cues. Breastfed newborns usually nurse about every 2 hours, which is roughly 10 to 12 feeding sessions in 24 hours, according to the CDC. The WHO recommends exclusive breastfeeding for about the first six months, and the table below lists what the sources give for each age.

## How do I know my baby is getting enough?
Regular wet and dirty diapers are a sign that a baby is eating enough: a well-nourished newborn has 6 or more wet diapers a day by 5 to 7 days old, with pale urine, according to the CDC. The AAP says a baby may lose up to about 8 to 10 percent of birth weight in the first days after birth. Not gaining about 5 to 7 oz a week after the milk comes in is a warning sign to raise with a clinician. The CDC also lists breastfeeding fewer than 8 times in 24 hours on most days as a possible warning sign.

## How much does a baby drink at 6 months?
By 6 months, a baby takes about 6 to 8 oz (180 to 240 mL) at each of 4 or 5 feedings in 24 hours, according to the AAP. The WHO says that around six months a baby's energy and nutrient needs begin to exceed what breast milk provides, so complementary foods should be added while breastfeeding continues, up to two years or beyond. For when to start, see [when to start solid food](/feeding/when-to-start-solid-food/). The sources we checked do not give a verified milk amount for the rest of the first year, so ask your pediatrician about the mix that fits your baby.
