Many diaper rashes can be treated at home, but some signs need a doctor's call. This checklist covers the home steps first and the signs that mean you should contact the pediatrician.

## What should I do first for diaper rash?

For a simple irritant rash, the AAP advises rinsing the area gently with water, letting the skin air-dry, and applying a thick barrier cream. Kindergesundheit-Info also advises changing diapers as often as possible, and right after each bowel movement, to prevent skin inflammation. Use these steps at each change:

- [ ] Change the diaper as often as possible, and right after each bowel movement. See [how often to change a diaper](/diapering/how-often-to-change-a-diaper/).
- [ ] Rinse the area gently with water.
- [ ] Let the skin air-dry before a new diaper goes on.
- [ ] Apply a thick layer of fragrance-free barrier cream, such as zinc oxide or petrolatum.

## Which barrier cream should I use for diaper rash?

Zinc oxide and petrolatum are both good barrier-cream choices, and fragrance-free products are best. Apply a thick layer to the diaper area after bathing. Kindergesundheit-Info also lists zinc oxide ointment in the basic home first-aid kit for children, for a sore bottom.

## Can I give my baby ibuprofen for diaper rash?

The AAP advises avoiding ibuprofen for babies under 6 months of age. For an older baby, ask your pediatrician before giving any medicine for the rash.

## How long does diaper rash take to clear?

A simple diaper rash should clear in about three days when it is treated correctly. If it does not, yeast may be involved, and the AAP suggests an over-the-counter anti-yeast cream.

## When should I call the pediatrician about diaper rash?

Call the doctor now for bright red skin that peels off in sheets, for fever with an infected look or spreading redness, and for any concern in a baby under one month old. Call within 24 hours for pimples, blisters, boils, yellow scabs, or open sores in the diaper area. Any diaper rash that comes with a fever also warrants a call to the doctor. Contact the doctor during office hours if the rash spreads outside the diaper area or has not improved after three days of anti-yeast cream.
