Most diaper rashes are mild and clear up at home within a few days. A few signs mean it is time to call the pediatrician, and the table below shows which ones and how soon.

## What causes diaper rash?

Most diaper rash is irritant rash, caused by skin sitting in contact with urine and stool. Diarrhea makes it more likely, because loose stool irritates the skin. Yeast is the other common cause: a yeast rash is bright red with sharp edges, is usually worst in the skin folds of the groin, and can follow a course of antibiotics.

## What should I do first for diaper rash?

Change the diaper as often as you can, and right after every poop, so stool does not stay on the skin. At each change, rinse the area gently with water and let the skin air-dry before the next diaper goes on. Then apply a thick layer of barrier cream to protect the skin. Our page on [how often to change a diaper](/diapering/how-often-to-change-a-diaper/) covers the daily routine.

## Which diaper cream should I use?

Zinc oxide and petrolatum are both good choices, and fragrance-free products are best for irritated skin. Apply a thick layer, including after a bath, so the cream forms a barrier between the skin and the diaper. Zinc oxide ointment is also a staple of a basic home first-aid kit for children.

## How long does diaper rash take to clear?

A diaper rash treated this way should clear in about three days. If it has not, yeast is a likely cause, and an over-the-counter anti-yeast cream is the next step. If the rash is still not better after three days of anti-yeast cream, call your pediatrician.

## Should I give my baby medicine for diaper rash?

Do not give ibuprofen to a baby under 6 months old. For an older baby, ask your pediatrician before giving any medicine for the rash. Barrier cream and frequent changes are the main treatment.

## When should I call the pediatrician about diaper rash?

Call right away for skin that peels off in sheets, for fever, or for any worry in a baby under one month old. Most other warning signs can wait for a call within a day or during office hours.

| Sign | When to call |
|---|---|
| Bright red skin that peels off in sheets | Now |
| Fever, especially with spreading redness or an infected look | Now |
| Any concern in a baby under 1 month | Now |
| Pimples, blisters, boils, yellow scabs or open sores | Within 24 hours |
| Rash spreading beyond the diaper area | During office hours |
| No improvement after 3 days of anti-yeast cream | During office hours |
