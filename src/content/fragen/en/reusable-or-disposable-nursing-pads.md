If your breasts leak between feeds, a nursing pad keeps your bra and clothes dry. The choice between washable and disposable comes down to how you live, not to which one works better.

## Do you need nursing pads?
You need them if your breasts leak between feedings, and they are most useful if you leak a lot. A small pack in your [hospital bag](/checklists/hospital-bag/) lets you see how much you leak before you stock up.

## What is the difference between washable and disposable pads?
Both slip inside the bra cup and absorb milk the same way; the difference is what happens afterward. Disposable pads go in the trash, which is easy on busy days and when you are out. Washable pads go in the laundry and are used again, so you produce less waste but need enough to rotate while a set is being washed.

| | Disposable pads | Washable pads |
|---|---|---|
| After use | Throw away | Wash and reuse |
| Convenience | Easy when out or traveling | Need a laundry routine and a spare set |
| Waste | New pads every day | Less waste |
| Watch out for | Plastic liners that hold moisture in | Plastic liners that block air flow |

## Which nursing pads should you avoid?
Avoid pads with plastic liners. They stop air from circulating around the nipples and hold moisture in, which can irritate the skin. Check the packaging or product description for a plastic liner before you buy.

## How do you use nursing pads?
Place a pad inside each bra cup and change it once it feels damp, so wet fabric does not sit against your skin. Many parents mix both types: washable pads at home and disposables for outings or nights away. Wash reusable pads with the rest of your laundry, following the care label; the tips in [how to wash baby clothes](/clothing/how-to-wash-baby-clothes/) work for them too.
