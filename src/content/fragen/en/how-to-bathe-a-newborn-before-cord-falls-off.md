In the first weeks, a newborn is washed with a washcloth instead of in a tub. A sponge bath takes a few minutes, keeps the cord stump dry and is easy once you have everything within reach.

## When does the umbilical cord fall off?

The cord stump usually falls off between 1 and 3 weeks after birth. Keep giving sponge baths until then and for a few more days after. Once the belly button has healed, your baby is ready for a first tub bath.

## How do you give a sponge bath step by step?

Gather everything first, then lay your baby on a padded, flat surface such as a changing pad. Keep one hand on your baby at all times, or use the safety strap. Wash the face first, keeping water out of the eyes and mouth, then the body, and finish with the diaper area. Keep your baby wrapped in a dry towel and uncover only the part you are washing.

## Which spots need extra attention?

The skin folds collect milk, sweat and lint. Pay special attention to the creases under the arms, behind the ears, around the neck and in the genital area, and pat them dry afterward.

## How do you keep the cord stump clean and dry?

Keep it clean and dry. Fold the top of the diaper down below the stump so urine does not soak it, and look at the stump at every diaper change. For how often that is in the newborn weeks, see [how often to change a diaper](/diapering/how-often-to-change-a-diaper/).

## When should you call the doctor about the cord?

Call your baby's doctor if you see yellow or white pus, or swelling or redness around the belly button. Call right away if the cord actively bleeds. A few drops of blood on the diaper around the time the stump falls off are normal.

### Sponge bath checklist

- [ ] Gather supplies first: a basin of warm water, a soft washcloth, a dry towel, a clean diaper and clothes.
- [ ] Lay your baby on a padded, flat surface.
- [ ] Keep one hand on your baby, or use the safety strap.
- [ ] Keep your baby wrapped in the towel and uncover one part at a time.
- [ ] Wash the face first, keeping water out of the eyes and mouth.
- [ ] Wash the body, including the creases under the arms, behind the ears, around the neck and in the genital area.
- [ ] Finish with the diaper area.
- [ ] Fold the diaper below the cord stump and check the stump at every change.
- [ ] Call the doctor for pus, swelling or redness around the belly button; call right away for active bleeding.
