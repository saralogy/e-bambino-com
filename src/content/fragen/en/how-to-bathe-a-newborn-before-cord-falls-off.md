A newborn gets sponge baths, not tub baths, until the umbilical cord stump falls off. Gathering everything first and keeping the baby wrapped and supported makes washing simpler in these early weeks.

## When does the umbilical cord usually fall off?

The cord stump usually falls off between 1 and 3 weeks after birth. Keep giving sponge baths until it falls off, and for a few more days after that. Move to a tub bath once the umbilical area has healed. Before a baby can use a tub, see [baby bathtub or bath seat: what to check before buying](/bath-and-care/baby-bathtub-or-bath-seat/). For how often to bathe a baby, see [how often to bathe a baby](/bath-and-care/how-often-to-bathe-a-baby/).

## How do you give a sponge bath step by step?

Start by gathering all supplies, and lay the baby on a padded, flat surface. Use a safety strap or keep one hand on the baby. Wash the face first, keeping water out of the eyes and mouth, then the body, and finish with the diaper area. Keep the baby wrapped in a dry towel, exposing only the part being washed.

## How do you keep the umbilical cord area clean and dry?

Keep the stump clean and dry, and fold the diaper below the cord so urine does not soak it. Check the stump at each diaper change. Kindergesundheit-info, a German source, says a baby may be bathed before the navel has healed if there are no signs of inflammation. If there are signs of inflammation, use a washcloth instead.

## Which signs mean you should call a doctor?

Call the baby's doctor for yellow or white pus, or for swelling or redness around the belly button. Call right away if the cord actively bleeds. A few drops of blood on the diaper around the time the stump falls off are normal. This list follows AAP guidance, so ask your pediatrician about any other signs to watch for.

### Sponge bath and cord care checklist

- [ ] Gather all supplies first, including a dry towel to wrap the baby in.
- [ ] Lay the baby on a padded, flat surface.
- [ ] Use a safety strap, or keep one hand on the baby.
- [ ] Wash the face first, keeping water out of the eyes and mouth.
- [ ] Wash the body, paying attention to the creases under the arms, behind the ears, around the neck and in the genital area.
- [ ] Finish with the diaper area. Keep the baby wrapped in a dry towel, exposing only the part you are washing.
- [ ] Keep the stump clean and dry, and fold the diaper below the cord.
- [ ] Check the cord at every diaper change.
- [ ] Call the doctor for yellow or white pus, swelling or redness around the belly button, or active bleeding from the cord.
