The basic difference is the cut: a one-piece outfit or a base layer. Everything else follows from that, for changing, sleeping and sizing.

## What does each one cover?

A footed sleeper (also called a sleepsuit or footie) covers the torso, arms, legs and usually the feet, and opens with snaps or a zip down the front or legs. A bodysuit covers the torso, has short or long sleeves, and closes with snaps at the crotch. It is worn on its own in warm weather or under pants, sleepers and sweaters.

## Which is easier for diaper changes?

Look for openings rather than the type. Sleepers with a two-way zip or snaps down the legs make night changes quick. Bodysuits with envelope necklines can be pulled down over the body after a messy diaper instead of over the head.

## Which is better for nights?

A sleeper over a bodysuit is the usual night outfit, combined with a sleep sack suited to the room temperature. In warm rooms, a bodysuit alone inside a light sleep sack can be enough.

## How do they grow with my baby?

Bodysuits stretch and often fit for longer. Sleepers get too short in the feet first: if your baby’s toes push against the end or the legs pull, move up a size.

| Criterion | Footed sleeper | Bodysuit |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage | Whole body, often feet | Torso only |
| Warmth | Higher | Lower, depends on layers |
| Changing | Snaps or zip on the legs | Snaps at the crotch |
| Use | Nights, cold days | Base layer all year |
| Fit over time | Feet get tight first | Stretches, often lasts longer |
