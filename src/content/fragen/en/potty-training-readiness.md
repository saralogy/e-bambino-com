Official sources describe toddler readiness in physical, mental, and willingness terms rather than as one age. Use this checklist to see where your toddler stands.

## What are the signs a toddler is ready for potty training?

A toddler is ready when they stay dry for at least two hours at a time during the day, or stay dry after naps, and notice their own bladder and bowel signals. Check each sign you have seen in your toddler.

- [ ] Stays dry for at least two hours at a time during the day, or stays dry after naps.
- [ ] Notices their own bladder and bowel signals, such as grunting, squatting, or freezing.
- [ ] Follows simple instructions and tells an adult when they need help getting to the toilet.
- [ ] Can walk to and from the bathroom, help undress, sit or stand comfortably, and pull pants up and down.
- [ ] Dislikes wet diapers, asks to be changed, asks to use the potty, or asks for big-kid underwear.

## Is there a right age to start potty training?

There is no single right age. The average age toilet training begins in the United States is between 2 and 3 years, and most children are bowel and bladder trained by age 4, according to the AAP. Kindergesundheit-Info says bladder and bowel control usually comes once the child is physically ready, with first signs in the second and third year, and that early potty training does not make it faster.

## Do I need a potty chair or a seat insert?

Official sources we checked do not say whether to use a potty chair or a seat insert, so this guide does not recommend one. The AAP lists sitting and standing comfortably among the physical signs of readiness, so choose the option your toddler can use comfortably.

## How long does potty training take?

No official source we checked gives a typical length in days or weeks, so this guide gives no number. The CDC warns that starting too early can cause stress and setbacks, which can make training take longer.

## Is nighttime bedwetting normal during potty training?

Yes. Kindergesundheit-Info considers occasional nighttime bedwetting up to age 5 to be normal. Nighttime bowel control often comes early and naturally, while bladder control usually comes much later, according to the AAP. For the AAP's guidance on training pants and diapers at night, see [pull-ups vs. tape diapers](/diapering/pull-ups-vs-tape-diapers/).

## What if constipation starts during potty training?

The AAP warns that constipation can start a withholding cycle during training. Keep trying and keep any prescribed medicine until training is complete, and have a pediatrician check the constipation rather than stopping training on your own.
