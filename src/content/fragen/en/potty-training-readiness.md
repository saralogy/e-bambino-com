Bladder and bowel control develop on their own schedule, and no amount of practice makes the body ready sooner. Watching for the signs below saves you and your toddler a lot of frustration.

## What are the signs a toddler is ready for potty training?

A toddler is ready when their body, understanding and willingness all line up. Most children show several of these signs before training goes smoothly:

- [ ] Stays dry for at least two hours at a time during the day, or wakes up dry after naps.
- [ ] Notices the urge to go, for example by grunting, squatting or going still before a poop.
- [ ] Follows simple instructions and can tell you when they need help getting to the toilet.
- [ ] Walks to and from the bathroom, sits comfortably and helps pull pants down and up.
- [ ] Dislikes wet diapers, asks to be changed, or asks to use the potty or wear big-kid underwear.

## Is there a right age to start potty training?

There is no single right age. In the US, toilet training usually begins between 2 and 3 years, and most children are bowel and bladder trained by age 4. First signs of readiness typically show up in the second and third year, and starting earlier does not make a child dry sooner.

## What happens if you start too early?

Starting before a child is ready tends to cause stress and setbacks, which can make training take longer overall. If your toddler resists or has many accidents, it is fine to pause and try again once you see more readiness signs.

## Should I use a potty chair or a seat on the toilet?

Either can work. A potty chair is child-sized, so your toddler's feet can rest on the floor. If your toddler uses the regular toilet, a child seat attachment helps, and a step stool gives their feet something to brace against.

## Should my toddler wear training pants or underwear?

Once training starts, switch to big-kid underwear during the day. The AAP notes that diapers and disposable training pants send a toddler the message that they do not need to use the toilet. Some parents use thicker cloth training pants to protect clothing; see [pull-ups vs. tape diapers](/diapering/pull-ups-vs-tape-diapers/) for how the disposable options compare.

## Is bedwetting normal after potty training?

Yes. Bowel control at night often comes early, while nighttime bladder control usually comes much later than daytime dryness. Occasional bedwetting up to age 5 is considered normal, and a training pant or diaper at night is fine for as long as your child is comfortable in it.

## What if my toddler gets constipated during potty training?

Have your pediatrician check it. Constipation makes pooping hurt, which can lead a toddler to hold it in, and that cycle can stall training. Follow any treatment your pediatrician prescribes until training is complete.
