This guide compares a bassinet, a bedside crib and a full crib. The safety rules are the same for all three, so check those rules first, then compare size and space. A bedside crib, also called a bedside sleeper, attaches to an adult bed.

## Which option is safe for a newborn from the first night?
Official guidance does not say which option is usable from the first night, so check each product's labeling. All three share the same safe setup: a firm, flat surface that meets federal standards, with only a fitted sheet. The AAP also recommends keeping the bassinet or crib near your bed, not in it.

| Option | Usable from | Usable until | Space needed | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bassinet | Check the manufacturer's labeling | Check the manufacturer's stated limits | Measure the spot and check dimensions | Must meet federal standards; check for recalls |
| Bedside crib (bedside sleeper) | Check the manufacturer's labeling | Check the manufacturer's stated limits | Check dimensions and how it attaches to your bed | Some convertible bedside bassinets were recalled in 2026 because the bed attachment left a gap, creating a fall hazard |
| Full crib | Check the manufacturer's labeling | Check the manufacturer's stated limits | Measure the spot and check dimensions | Must meet federal standards; check for recalls |

## How long can you use each one before it is too small?
No official source we reviewed gives weight, height or age limits for these options, so check the stated limits in each product's manual. Stop using the product once your baby reaches them.

## What does each option cost and how much space does it take?
We have not verified prices or floor space for these options, so this guide does not compare them. Measure the spot where the baby will sleep and check each product's dimensions before you buy. If floor space is tight, see [nursery in a small apartment](/nursery/nursery-in-a-small-apartment/).

## Which sleep products should parents avoid?
Parents should avoid inclined sleep products, which federal law bans, and any sleep product that does not meet the CPSC mandatory standard for infant sleep products. That standard requires a sleep surface angle of 10 degrees or lower. Between 2005 and mid-2019, the CPSC counted 1,108 incidents, including 73 infant deaths, linked to inclined infant sleep products. In 2026, the CPSC warned consumers to stop using a set of travel bassinets, and recalled convertible bedside bassinets because of a fall hazard. If a bed-in-a-bag set adds bumpers, pillows or blankets, those do not belong in the sleep space under the CPSC's bare sleep space guidance. For what does belong, see [crib bedding for a baby's first year](/nursery/bedding-for-baby-first-year/).
