The safety rules are the same for all three, so the real choice is about space, budget and how long you want to use it. A bedside sleeper is a bassinet that sits flush against the side of your bed.

## Is a bassinet safer than a crib for a newborn?

No, both are equally safe when they meet federal safety standards. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) counts cribs, bassinets, play yards and bedside sleepers as safe sleep products, as long as the baby sleeps on a firm, flat surface with only a fitted sheet. Whichever you choose, the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) recommends placing it in your room, near your bed but not in it.

| Option | Usable until | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bassinet | About 5 months, or once your baby can push up on hands and knees | Small rooms, keeping baby within arm's reach | A short lifespan; follow the weight limit on the label |
| Bedside sleeper | About 5 months, or once your baby can push up on hands and knees | Night feeds right next to your bed | The attachment must leave no gap between the sleeper and your mattress |
| Full crib | Until your child can climb out or is about 35 inches (89 cm) tall | One purchase that lasts into the toddler years | Takes the most floor space |

## When does a baby outgrow a bassinet or bedside sleeper?

At about 5 months, or as soon as your baby can push up on hands and knees, whichever comes first. That is the cutoff the federal safety standards for bassinets and bedside sleepers set. The manufacturer's weight limit on the label also applies, so stop at whichever limit your baby reaches first.

## How long does a crib last?

A crib lasts until your child can climb out or reaches about 35 inches (89 cm) tall. That makes a crib the better value if you have the room for it from day one. For signs your child has outgrown it, see [how long a baby uses a crib](/nursery/how-long-does-a-crib-last/).

## Which one fits a small bedroom?

A bassinet or bedside sleeper takes the least floor space, which is why many parents start with one and move to a crib later. Measure the spot next to your bed and compare it with the product's listed dimensions before you buy. If space is tight everywhere, see [how to set up a nursery in a small apartment](/nursery/nursery-in-a-small-apartment/).

## Which baby sleep products should I avoid?

Avoid inclined sleepers and anything that does not meet the federal standard for infant sleep products, which allows a sleep surface angle of no more than 10 degrees. Federal law bans inclined sleep products and padded crib bumpers outright. Check the CPSC recall list before you buy or accept a used bassinet or crib, because recalls continue to happen, including bedside sleepers whose bed attachment left a dangerous gap.
