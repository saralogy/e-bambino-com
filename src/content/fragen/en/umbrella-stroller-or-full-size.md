Umbrella and full-size strollers suit different stages and different trips. This page compares them on newborn use, weight, folded size, and the age range each manufacturer states.

## Can you use an umbrella stroller from birth?
Generally not for a newborn. The CPSC defines a carriage as a wheeled vehicle generally used to transport an infant who is lying down, while strollers hold children sitting up or semi-reclined. A newborn who needs to lie flat needs a carriage-style product. Check [when a baby can use a stroller](/on-the-go/when-can-a-baby-use-a-stroller/) first. Do not put children to sleep in strollers: the AAP advises against it, especially for infants younger than 4 months, and the CPSC warns of a serious entrapment or strangulation risk, especially under 12 months. Verify any newborn lie-flat claim with the manufacturer.

## Is an umbrella stroller lighter and easier to fold for travel?
Yes, generally. The CPSC notes that umbrella strollers are the lightest and most basic type and fold compactly, though they may lack some accessories. Exact weights and folded sizes are set by each manufacturer, so compare the listed numbers for the exact models. Fold the stroller in a store, or measure your car trunk, before you buy it for travel. For a side-by-side with bundled options, see [travel system or separate stroller](/on-the-go/travel-system-or-separate-stroller/).

## Which one handles rough ground and long walks?
No official source compares umbrella and full-size strollers on rough ground or long walks, so test each model on the surfaces you use most. Wheel size, suspension, and frame design are set by each manufacturer and differ by model. Check each spec sheet before you buy.

## Does an umbrella stroller work for a second child?
No official source covers this, so check with the manufacturer before you decide. Check each model's stated age and weight limits, and confirm them in writing if they matter for your plans.
