Some families end up with both: a full-size stroller for the first months and everyday walks, and an umbrella stroller for travel and quick trips once their baby sits up.

## Can I use an umbrella stroller from birth?

No, not for a young baby who needs to lie flat. The CPSC notes that umbrella stroller seats typically do not recline. The CPSC calls a product that carries a baby lying down a carriage; for a newborn, that means a stroller with a bassinet or a seat that reclines flat. For the full picture by age, see [when a baby can use a stroller](/on-the-go/when-can-a-baby-use-a-stroller/).

## When is my baby ready for an umbrella stroller?

When your baby can sit up steadily with good head control. The CDC lists sitting without support among the milestones most babies reach by 9 months, though some get there sooner. Until then, a stroller that reclines gives better support.

## Is an umbrella stroller better for travel?

Usually, yes. The CPSC describes umbrella strollers as the lightest and most basic type, compact when folded, though they may lack some of the accessories a full-size stroller has. Compare the listed weight and folded size of the exact models, and check the fold against your car trunk or overhead storage.

## Which stroller handles rough ground and long walks?

It depends on the exact model more than the type. If you walk on bumpy paths or go on long walks, compare wheel size, suspension and frame on the spec sheets before you buy, and try the stroller on an uneven surface if you can.

## Can my baby nap in either stroller?

Not for routine sleep in either one. The AAP advises against letting children sleep in strollers, especially babies younger than 4 months, and the CPSC warns of entrapment and strangulation risk, especially under 12 months. If your baby falls asleep on a walk, move them to a firm, flat crib or bassinet when you get home. In any stroller, use the harness every time and set the brake when you stop.
