## What is the difference between a baby tub, a bath seat and a sink insert?

An infant bath tub is a separate product from a bath seat, which is an add-on that sits inside a large adult bathtub. Under U.S. rules, infant bath tubs and bath seats are separate product categories, and each has its own mandatory federal safety standard. The federal standard for infant bath tubs took effect on October 2, 2017, and sellers cannot legally sell non-compliant tubs made on or after that date. The sources we checked do not cover sink inserts, so this page makes no safety claims about them.

## From what age and weight does each option work?

The sources we checked do not confirm age or weight ranges for any of these options. Check the manufacturer's label for each product, and do not rely on a limit you have not confirmed there.

## Does a bath seat replace adult supervision?

No. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) says bath seats and rings are not safety devices and do not replace adult supervision. CPSC data for 2004 to 2015 counted 247 incidents with infant bathtubs, 31 of them fatal, and a parent or guardian was not present in 30 of those 31 deaths. A baby should never be left alone in the bath, even for a moment. For bath frequency, see [how often to bathe a baby](/bath-and-care/how-often-to-bathe-a-baby/).

## What should parents check before buying a baby bathtub or bath seat?

Check the manufacture date on infant tubs, and confirm that a bath seat is not on a CPSC warning or recall list. Look for a stable, non-slip setup: kindergesundheit-info says a baby tub needs a non-slip insert, and an add-on seat in a large tub must not wobble, tip or slip. Also check for leg openings that could trap a child, a known problem with some bath seats. Kindergesundheit-info says a baby tub should be filled only halfway with water, because a child can drown in as little as 10 cm of water if the head goes under, even if the child can already sit.
