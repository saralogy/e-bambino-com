A baby bathtub and a bath seat solve different problems at different ages. A tub holds a young baby who cannot sit yet; a bath seat props up an older baby in the big tub. Both are covered by mandatory U.S. federal safety standards.

| | Baby bathtub | Bath seat |
|---|---|---|
| Made for | Young babies who cannot sit up yet | Babies who can sit, typically marketed for about 5 to 10 months |
| Where it is used | On its own, or inside an adult tub or sink | Inside an adult tub or sink, giving back and front support |
| Federal standard | Mandatory since October 2, 2017 | Mandatory, with stricter stability and leg-opening rules |
| Known hazards | Drowning when a baby is left alone; falls | Tipping over, legs getting trapped in the openings, climbing out |
| Check before buying | Manufacture date; a sloped, textured or non-slip surface; the age and weight limits on the label | Not on a CPSC warning or recall list; stands firmly without wobbling, tipping or slipping; the age and weight limits on the label |

## Which one does a newborn need?

A baby bathtub. The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP), citing the CPSC, recommends a hard plastic baby tub with a sloped, textured surface or a sling that keeps a young baby from sliding, made on or after October 2, 2017. Your newborn will get sponge baths until the cord stump falls off, so the tub comes into use after the first weeks.

## When can a baby use a bath seat?

Only once your baby can sit up. The federal standard covers seats that give back and front support to a seated baby, and they are typically marketed for babies of about 5 to 10 months. Always follow the age, weight and developmental limits printed on the seat, since they differ by model.

## Is a bath seat a safety device?

No. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) says bath seats and rings are not safety devices and do not replace adult supervision. Its incident data for 2004 to 2015 counted 247 incidents with infant bathtubs, 31 of them fatal; in 30 of those 31 deaths, no parent or caregiver was present. Keep one hand on your baby, and never leave them alone in the bath, not even for a moment.

## What should you check before buying?

For an infant tub, check the manufacture date: tubs made on or after October 2, 2017 must meet the federal standard, which covers latching and locking, load testing and drowning and fall warnings. For a bath seat, search the model on the CPSC recall and warning lists, because CPSC has warned about seats that tip over or have leg openings that can trap a child. Either way, the product should sit firmly without wobbling or slipping, and a secondhand item deserves the same checks.

## How much water goes in a baby bathtub?

About 2 inches, according to the AAP. A baby can drown in 1 or 2 inches of water, so a shallow tub is never a reason to step away. If you are short on space, see [setting up a nursery in a small apartment](/nursery/nursery-in-a-small-apartment/).
