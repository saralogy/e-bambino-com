A nursery of its own is a nice-to-have, not a must. When space is tight, what counts is a safe, calm place to sleep and a setup that makes nights easier.

## What furniture do I really need at the start?

A crib, bassinet or bedside crib with a firm, flat mattress; a changing pad on a dresser or another stable surface at hip height; storage for diapers and clothes; and a dim light for night feeds. Everything else is comfort. In a very small room, a changing topper on an existing dresser replaces a full changing unit.

## Where should the crib stand?

In the first months, in your bedroom, where you can hear and reach your baby. Keep it away from windows, blind cords, radiators and heaters, and out of reach of shelves, lamps and anything your baby could pull in. A nook with curtains or canopies around the bed is not a good spot, because air cannot circulate well.

## How do I find storage without big wardrobes?

Use wall space above head height for things you need less often, and keep daily items low and within reach. Baskets in an open shelf often replace a wardrobe: one for diapers, one for bodysuits, one for sleep sacks. Anchor dressers and shelves to the wall, because a toddler who climbs can tip them over.

| Space | Use | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Top of a dresser | Changing pad, daily essentials | Anchor the dresser to the wall |
| Lower drawers or baskets | Diapers, clothes, care products | Easy to reach with one hand |
| High shelf | Spare stock, rarely used items | Out of your child’s reach |
| Floor near a wall | Play mat | Away from cords and windows |
