Age is not the rule that matters most. The limits printed on your car seat are.

## Why is rear-facing safer?

In a frontal crash, a rear-facing seat spreads the forces across the child’s back and head instead of letting the head whip forward. A young child’s neck is not yet strong enough to take that load, which is why safety organizations recommend staying rear-facing as long as possible.

## What do the rules say?

Seats approved under UN R129 (i-Size) must be used rear-facing until at least 15 months. Older R44 seats use weight groups instead. Whatever the approval, the seat’s manual and labels set the binding limits.

## What comes after a forward-facing harness seat?

A high-back booster that positions the adult belt correctly across the shoulder and hips. Many countries require a child restraint until a set height or age; in Germany that is 150 cm or 12 years. Check the rules in your country.

Rules and seat limits change. Always follow your seat’s manual and your local law.
