How long a crib lasts depends less on your child’s age than on two things: whether they can climb out, and how tall they are.

## How do I know my child has outgrown the crib?

The clearest sign is climbing: once your child gets a leg over the rail, a fall from crib height is a real risk, even with the mattress at its lowest. Height matters too. When your child is about 89 cm (35 in) tall, the rail is usually too low relative to their body to keep them in safely.

## What should I check on a crib?

A firm, flat mattress that fits snugly, with no gap wider than two fingers at the sides. Bars no more than about 6 cm apart so a head cannot get through. A mattress base you can lower once your baby can sit up and pull to stand. No cut-outs, loose parts or peeling paint.

## What comes after the crib?

Many cribs convert into a toddler bed by removing one side. Otherwise, a low toddler bed or a standard bed with a guard rail are common next steps. Choose a mattress size that matches the bed exactly.

| Stage | Typical bed | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 6 months | Bassinet, bedside crib or crib | Firm, flat mattress, nothing else in the bed |
| Sitting and standing | Crib with lowered base | Lower the mattress before your baby can pull up |
| Climbing or about 89 cm tall | Toddler bed or bed with rail | Low height, soft landing area |

These are guidelines to help you judge a crib. Always follow the manufacturer’s limits for your model.
