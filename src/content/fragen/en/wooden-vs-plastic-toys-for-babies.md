Wood and plastic each carry different risks, so the product and its safety labels matter as much as the material. This page draws on German consumer and child-health guidance and US rules, and says where the sources are silent.

## Is one material safer for babies who mouth their toys?
The sources do not rank wood and plastic for mouthing. The main chemical concern named is soft plastic: Verbraucherzentrale warns that cheap no-name products may contain banned plasticizers, and polycarbonate can release bisphenol A or other bisphenols. Verbraucherzentrale prefers untreated solid wood, and CPSC lead limits of 100 ppm in accessible parts and 90 ppm in paint apply to children's products whatever the material.

## Which material lasts longer and is easier to clean?
The sources used for this page do not compare durability or cleaning between wood and plastic, so this page makes no claim either way. For wooden toys, Kindergesundheit-info advises unpainted or waxed wood and regular checks for cracks, splinters and sharp edges.

## What do CE marking and the EN 71 toy standard tell you?
The CE mark on toys sold in the EU is a manufacturer's declaration, not an independent test mark, and toys without a CE mark may not be placed on the EU market. The GS mark indicates voluntary, independent third-party testing commissioned by the manufacturer, and the wording 'Sicherheit und Schadstoffe' (safety and pollutants) matters for chemical safety. Painted or lacquered wooden toys should meet the German toy standard DIN EN 71-3, which the sources reviewed here mention without detail; the full EN 71 standard is not covered by the sources used.

## Which materials suit teething and first grasping toys?
The sources do not confirm which materials suit teething, so this page makes no material recommendation for it. Choking is the risk they do address: under CPSC small-parts rules, toys for children under 3 must not have small parts that fit in a test cylinder about 2.25 by 1.25 inches. CPSC teether recalls in 2026 cited pull-string teethers for choking risk under the mandatory toy standard. For first grasping toys, see [toys for motor skills in the first year](/play/toys-for-motor-skills-first-year/).
