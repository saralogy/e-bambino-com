Babies explore everything with their mouths, so what a toy is made of matters. Both wood and plastic can be safe or unsafe, and a few simple checks tell you which kind you are holding.

## Which material is safer for a baby who mouths toys?

The safest choice is untreated or waxed solid wood, or a hard, PVC-free plastic such as polypropylene (PP) or polyethylene (PE). German consumer advisers see soft plastic as the bigger chemical concern: cheap no-name products may contain banned plasticizers, and PVC often contains harmful ones. Polycarbonate can release bisphenol A or other bisphenols, so it is best avoided for toys that go in the mouth.

## What should I look out for with wooden toys?

Choose unpainted or waxed solid wood where you can. If wood is painted or lacquered, the finish should meet toy safety standards, and plywood puzzles have failed tests more often because they can release formaldehyde. Check wooden toys regularly for cracks, splinters and sharp edges, and put away any that are damaged.

## What should I look out for with plastic toys?

Choose hard plastic labeled PVC-free or phthalate-free, ideally PP or PE. Be wary of soft, squishy plastic from unknown brands and of anything with a strong chemical smell. In the US, toys and child care articles may contain no more than 0.1% of any regulated phthalate in parts a child can touch.

| | Wood | Plastic |
|---|---|---|
| Good choice | Untreated or waxed solid wood | Hard, PVC-free plastic such as PP or PE |
| Be careful with | Painted or lacquered finishes; plywood, which can release formaldehyde | Soft plastic from unknown brands; PVC; polycarbonate, which can release bisphenols |
| Check regularly for | Cracks, splinters, sharp edges | Cracks, loose or broken parts |
| US rules that apply | Lead: max. 100 ppm in accessible parts, 90 ppm in paint | Lead limits plus max. 0.1% per regulated phthalate |

## What do safety labels on toys tell me?

In the US, children's products must stay within federal limits for lead (100 ppm in accessible parts and 90 ppm in paint) and phthalates, whatever they are made of. In the EU, every toy must carry the CE mark, but it is the manufacturer's own declaration, not an independent test. The German GS mark means the toy was tested by an independent lab; the wording "Sicherheit und Schadstoffe" (safety and pollutants) shows that chemicals were tested too.

## Which toys are safe for teething and first grasping?

Official guidance here is about choking rather than material: the toy must be large enough not to be swallowed and must have no loose parts or strings that can come off. Toys for children under 3 must not have small parts that fit entirely into a test cylinder of about 2.25 by 1.25 inches, and the US Consumer Product Safety Commission has recalled teethers with pull strings because of choking risk.
