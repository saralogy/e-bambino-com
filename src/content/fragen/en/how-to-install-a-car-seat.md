A car seat works best when it is installed tightly and at the correct angle. These steps cover the most important points in the seat's manual and in official guidance.

## Should the car seat face backward or forward?
Keep infants and toddlers in a rear-facing car seat as long as the seat allows. The AAP recommends this until they reach the highest weight or height set by the seat's manufacturer, not until a set age. A rear-facing car seat must sit at the correct recline angle so the child rides semi-reclined, which keeps the airway open. For the step to forward-facing, see [when your child can face forward in the car](/on-the-go/when-can-my-child-face-forward-in-the-car/).

## How tight should a car seat be with a seat belt or LATCH?
A correctly installed car seat should not move more than about 1 inch side-to-side or front-to-back at the belt path when you pull on it. With a seat belt, lock the belt, press firmly down on the seat while you tighten the belt, then check that it moves no more than about 1 inch. With lower anchors (LATCH), press down while you tighten the straps and apply the same 1-inch check. ISOFIX uses two standardized anchor points, which reduces the risk of incorrect installation, but the seat's manual still governs.

## Where in the car should the seat go?
Install the car seat in the back seat. The CDC says a rear-facing car seat should never be placed in front of an active airbag, because front passenger airbags can injure or kill young children. Follow the harness and chest clip placement in the checklist below.

## Is a car seat installation check worth it?
Yes. Certified Child Passenger Safety Technicians inspect car seats free of charge in most cases and can show you how to install and use the seat. NHTSA reports that almost half of all car seats are installed incorrectly, even though most parents and caregivers believe they know how to install them. NHTSA's Car Seat Inspection Finder locates inspection stations and virtual inspectors.

- [ ] Seat belt or lower anchors locked, and the seat moves no more than about 1 inch at the belt path when pulled
- [ ] Recline set to the angle the manual gives, so a rear-facing child rides semi-reclined
- [ ] Harness straps lie flat and run through the slots at or below the child's shoulders (rear-facing)
- [ ] Chest clip placed at armpit level
- [ ] Car seat in the back seat, away from an active front airbag
