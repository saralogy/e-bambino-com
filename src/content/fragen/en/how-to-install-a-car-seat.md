A car seat only protects your child when it is installed tightly, at the right angle, with the harness snug. NHTSA reports that almost half of all car seats are installed incorrectly, so a few minutes of checking is worth it.

## Should I use the seat belt or the lower anchors?

Use one or the other, as your car seat manual and your vehicle manual describe; both are safe when done correctly. Do not use both at the same time unless both manuals say you can. With a seat belt, lock the belt, press firmly down on the car seat and tighten. With lower anchors (LATCH, called ISOFIX in Europe), press down while you tighten the anchor straps. ISOFIX uses two standardized anchor points, which makes mistakes less likely.

## How tight should a car seat be?

Tight enough that it moves no more than about 1 inch (2.5 cm) side to side or front to back. Test it by pulling at the belt path, where the belt or anchor strap runs through the seat, not at the top of the seat. If it moves more, press down harder and tighten again.

## Which way should the car seat face?

Rear-facing, for as long as your seat allows. The AAP recommends keeping infants and toddlers rear-facing until they reach the highest weight or height the manufacturer allows, not until a set age; those limits are on the seat's label and in the manual. In Europe, i-Size seats (UN R129) must be used rear-facing until at least about 15 months. When the time comes, see [when your child can face forward in the car](/on-the-go/when-can-my-child-face-forward-in-the-car/).

## What recline angle does a rear-facing seat need?

The angle the manual gives for your child's age and weight. Rear-facing babies need to ride semi-reclined so their airway stays open. Follow the manual's instructions for setting and checking the angle.

## Where in the car should the car seat go?

In the back seat. Never place a rear-facing car seat in front of an active airbag: the CDC warns that front passenger airbags can injure or kill young children.

## How snug should the harness be?

Snug enough that you cannot pinch any extra strap material at your child's shoulder. For rear-facing seats, the straps lie flat and come through the slots at or below the shoulders, and the chest clip sits at armpit level. Bulky coats keep the harness from fitting snugly, so buckle your child in first and lay a coat or blanket over the harness.

### Car seat installation checklist

- [ ] Car seat in the back seat, never in front of an active airbag
- [ ] Installed with the seat belt (locked) or the lower anchors, not both unless both manuals allow it
- [ ] Moves no more than about 1 inch at the belt path when pulled
- [ ] Rear-facing seat set to the recline angle in the manual
- [ ] Harness straps flat, at or below the shoulders (rear-facing)
- [ ] No extra strap material to pinch at the shoulder
- [ ] Chest clip at armpit level
- [ ] No bulky coat under the harness

Want a second pair of eyes? Certified Child Passenger Safety Technicians check car seats free of charge in most cases and show you how to install and use yours. NHTSA's Car Seat Inspection Finder lists inspection stations and virtual checks near you.
