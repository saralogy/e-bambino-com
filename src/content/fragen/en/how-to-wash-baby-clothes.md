Baby laundry piles up fast. Choosing the right temperature, detergent and drying method keeps fabrics soft and skin happy without extra work.

## Which temperature is right?

Start with the care label. Cotton bodysuits, sleepers, bedding, washcloths and cloth diapers can usually be washed at 60 °C, which is useful for heavily soiled items. For everyday light dirt, 40 °C is usually enough. Wool, silk and many fleece items need 30 °C and a gentle or wool program.

## Which detergent should I use?

A mild detergent without fragrance, dyes or optical brighteners is the safest choice for sensitive baby skin. Use the dose printed on the pack for your water hardness; more detergent does not clean better and leaves more residue. Skip fabric softener: it can irritate skin and makes towels and cloth diapers less absorbent.

## How do I get milk, poop and food stains out?

Rinse the stain from the back with cold water as soon as you can. Milk and food stains contain protein, which sets when it meets hot water. Soak stubborn stains for a while, then wash as usual. Drying in sunlight helps fade leftover poop stains on white cotton.

## How should baby clothes be dried?

Line drying is gentle and costs nothing. A tumble dryer on low heat keeps fleece and jersey soft, but check the label first, because some fabrics shrink. Close zips and snaps before washing so they do not snag other items.

### Temperature by fabric

| Fabric | Temperature | Program |
|---|---|---|
| Cotton, jersey | 40 to 60 °C | Normal or cotton |
| Fleece, synthetics | 30 to 40 °C | Easy-care or gentle |
| Wool, silk | 30 °C | Wool program, wool detergent |
| Bedding, towels, cloth diapers | 60 °C | Cotton |
