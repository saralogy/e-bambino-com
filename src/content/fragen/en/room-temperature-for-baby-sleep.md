Overheating is one of the risks that safe-sleep guidance names, so room temperature matters. Here is the target and how to keep your baby comfortable at night.

## What temperature is recommended for a baby's sleep room?
The German AWMF S1 guideline recommends 18 °C as the optimal nighttime room temperature. The German Kindergesundheit-info site (BZgA) also gives 18 degrees Celsius. The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) does not give a numeric range, but it calls for avoiding overheating during sleep.

| Check | What guidance says | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Room temperature | Aim for 18 °C at night (AWMF) | Check with a room thermometer |
| Covering | A sleep bag of suitable size is recommended instead of a duvet (AWMF) | Choose a sleep bag that fits, and skip the duvet |
| Loose blankets | A wearable blanket is preferable to loose blankets (AAP) | Dress your baby in a sleep sack instead |
| Overheating | Avoid overheating during sleep (AAP); keep the baby from getting too hot (NICHD) | Adjust clothing and covering so the baby does not get too hot |

## How do I tell if the room is too warm or too cold?
No official source we reviewed lists warning signs for a room that is too warm or too cold, so check the room against the 18 °C target with a thermometer. The AAP calls for avoiding overheating during sleep, so adjust clothing and covering to prevent it.

## How should I dress my baby for sleep at that temperature?
The AAP advises dressing your baby in layers or a wearable blanket instead of using loose blankets. A sleep sack should fit your baby and not cover the head. The AWMF guideline also recommends a sleep bag of suitable size instead of a duvet, which helps avoid overheating. For the sleep items themselves, see [crib bedding for a baby's first year](/nursery/bedding-for-baby-first-year/).

## How can I ventilate a nursery without a draft on the crib?
We could not confirm official guidance on ventilating a nursery, so this guide gives no steps for it. What the AAP does recommend is that the crib sit near the parents' bed, not in it, so the baby stays within view and arm's reach.
