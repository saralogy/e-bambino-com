Keeping your baby from getting too hot matters more than hitting an exact number. A cool room, light layers and a sleep sack do most of the work.

## What temperature should a baby's room be at night?

Aim for around 64 °F (18 °C). German safe-sleep guidelines name this as the ideal nighttime room temperature. The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) gives no exact number, because studies define overheating in different ways, but it is clear that overheating raises the risk of sudden infant death.

## How can I tell if my baby is too hot?

Feel your baby's chest. Sweating, flushed skin or a chest that feels hot to the touch are signs your baby is too warm. Take off a layer or switch to a lighter sleep sack, and check again after a few minutes.

| What you notice | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Sweating | Too warm | Remove a layer or use a lighter sleep sack |
| Flushed, red skin | Too warm | Remove a layer and cool the room |
| Chest feels hot to the touch | Too warm | Remove a layer and recheck soon |

## How should I dress my baby for sleep?

Dress your baby in no more than one layer more than you would need to be comfortable in the same room. A sleep sack or wearable blanket over sleep clothes keeps your baby warm without loose bedding that could cover the face. For choosing the right one, see [sleep sack or blanket](/clothing/sleep-sack-or-blanket/).

## Should my baby wear a hat to sleep?

No, not indoors. The AAP advises against hats for sleeping babies indoors, except in the first hours of life or in a neonatal intensive care unit (NICU), because a hat can make a baby too hot. Keep your baby's head and face uncovered during sleep.

## Do special mattresses or baby monitors help prevent overheating or SIDS?

No. German health guidance notes that special mattresses, sleep pads and home baby monitors do not lower the risk of sudden infant death and are not recommended for that purpose. What does help is the basics: back to sleep, a firm and flat sleep surface, a cool room and no loose bedding. For more, see the [baby sleep guide](/guides/baby-sleep/).
