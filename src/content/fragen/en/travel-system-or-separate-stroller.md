Both routes get you a stroller your infant car seat can click into. The real difference is how much choice you want, and how much checking you are willing to do yourself.

## What is a travel system?

A travel system is a stroller and an infant car seat sold together and designed to connect, so you can move a baby from the car to the stroller in the car seat. Some include a car seat base for the car and an adapter; others do not. Check the box and the manual for the exact parts included, because bundles with similar names can differ.

## Is a travel system cheaper than buying separately?

Sometimes, but it depends on the exact models and the retailer. Compare the bundle with the same stroller and car seat bought separately, on the same day, and add the price of any adapter the separate route needs. A bundle is only a saving if you would have chosen both parts anyway.

## How do I check that a car seat fits a stroller?

Use the model numbers. They are on labels on the stroller frame and on the car seat or its base; look them up on the stroller maker's compatibility list, and buy only the adapter listed for that exact pair. The CPSC has recalled stroller car seat adapters that could crack or fail to attach, and travel systems in which the car seat could come loose from the frame. Before you buy, and again if you buy secondhand, check the stroller and any adapter on the CPSC recall list and the car seat on NHTSA's recall lookup.

## When are a separate stroller and car seat the better choice?

When no bundle has both a car seat that fits your car and a stroller that suits your routes and storage. Buying separately lets you choose each piece on its own merits, for example a compact stroller for city trips or a car seat that fits well in a small back seat. The trade-off is that checking compatibility is up to you.
