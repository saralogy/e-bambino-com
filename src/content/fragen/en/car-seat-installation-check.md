A car seat protects a child only when it is installed tightly and at the right angle. Use the table below in your car, and the answers to understand the test behind each step. For safety checks inside the home, see [Dangerous Household Items for Toddlers](/safety/dangerous-household-items-for-toddlers/).

## How tight should a car seat be installed?

A correctly installed car seat should not move more than 1 inch side to side or front to back when you pull it at the belt path. NHTSA and CDC both use this one-inch limit. Press down firmly on the seat while you tighten the belt, as NHTSA advises.

## Which checks can I do myself at home?

You can check that the belt runs through the correct belt path with no twists, and that the buckle is locked. Use the pinch test at the shoulder: if you can pinch the harness straps, the harness is too loose. Rear-facing straps go at or below the shoulders, forward-facing straps at or above them, and the chest clip sits at armpit level.

## How reclined should a baby's car seat be?

Babies must ride semi-reclined to keep the airway open, and most infant seats have built-in angle indicators or adjusters to help set the recline. Our sources do not confirm specific recline angles for each seat type, so use the seat's own indicator.

## Where can I get a free car seat installation check?

NHTSA's Car Seat Inspection Finder locates inspection stations and virtual inspectors, and certified technicians inspect car seats free of charge in most cases. Our sources cannot confirm that a specific local station is open, so use the finder to check.

## Does a car seat need replacing after a crash?

NHTSA recommends replacing a car seat after a moderate or severe crash, and says a seat from such a crash should never be used again. A crash counts as minor only if the vehicle could be driven away, the door nearest the seat was undamaged, no one was injured, the airbags did not deploy and the seat shows no visible damage. Check the manufacturer's manual for its own crash rules.
