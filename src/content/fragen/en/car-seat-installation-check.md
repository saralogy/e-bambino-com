This is the quick inspection to run after you install a seat, and again from time to time, because belts loosen and children grow. In a 2009 NHTSA study that watched drivers install child seats and buckle children in, the most common mistakes were a chest clip at the wrong height (60%), the wrong recline (36.7%), twisted harness straps (30%) and a loose harness (20.7%). You can spot every one of them yourself in a few minutes.

## What is the 1-inch test?

It checks that the seat itself is tight. Hold the car seat at the belt path, where the seat belt or lower anchor strap runs through it, and pull side to side and front to back: it should move no more than 1 inch, the limit both NHTSA and CDC use. If it moves more, press down firmly on the seat and tighten the belt or strap again.

## What is the pinch test?

It checks that the harness is snug. With your child buckled in, try to pinch the strap webbing vertically at the shoulder; if you can pinch a fold, the harness is too loose, so tighten it until you can't. Buckle your child in first and put coats or blankets over the harness afterward, because bulky clothing keeps the harness from fitting snugly.

## Where should the harness straps and chest clip sit?

For a rear-facing seat, the straps come out at or below your child's shoulders; for a forward-facing seat, at or above them. The chest clip sits at armpit level, not down on the belly. Run your hand along each strap to make sure it lies flat with no twists.

## How do I check the recline angle?

Use the angle indicator built into the seat. Babies need to ride semi-reclined so their airway stays open, and the right angle depends on your seat, so follow the indicator and the manual rather than eyeballing it.

## What extra check does a forward-facing seat need?

The top tether. Connect it to the vehicle's tether anchor and pull it tight, as the manual shows. Keep your child rear-facing for as long as the seat allows before you turn it around; here is [when a child can face forward in the car](/on-the-go/when-can-my-child-face-forward-in-the-car/).

## When does a car seat need replacing?

After any moderate or severe crash: NHTSA says a seat from such a crash should never be used again. A crash counts as minor only if the car could be driven away, the door nearest the seat was undamaged, nobody was injured, the airbags did not deploy and the seat shows no visible damage. Register your seat with the manufacturer and check NHTSA's recall list so you hear about safety recalls.

## Where can I get a free car seat check?

Use NHTSA's Car Seat Inspection Finder to locate an inspection station or a virtual inspection near you. Certified child passenger safety technicians check seats free of charge in most cases, and they will show you how to fix anything that is off. It is a good step before your baby's first ride home or a long trip with the [family travel checklist](/checklists/family-travel/).

|---|---|---|
| 1-inch test | Pull the seat at the belt path, side to side and front to back | It moves no more than 1 inch |
| Belt path | Look at where the belt or anchor strap runs | It uses the correct path for the direction the seat faces, with no twists, and the belt is locked |
| Recline | Read the seat's angle indicator | The indicator shows the correct range for your child |
| Pinch test | Pinch the harness webbing at the shoulder | You cannot pinch a fold |
| Strap height | Look at where the straps leave the seat back | Rear-facing: at or below the shoulders; forward-facing: at or above |
| Chest clip | Check its height | It sits at armpit level |
| Top tether | Forward-facing seats only | It is attached to the tether anchor and tight |
