Reading aloud is the simplest way to support language there is. You need no practice and no expensive books. What matters is that you do it regularly, in your own voice.

## When can I start reading to a baby?

From the first weeks. Your baby hears your voice, recognizes it and links it with being close to you. In the first months, two or three short sessions a day are plenty. Hold your baby close so they can look at you or the page when they want to.

## How do I choose a book?

Look for clear, high-contrast pictures, only a few things on each page and a slow, simple rhythm. Board books with thick pages survive chewing and grabbing; touch-and-feel and lift-the-flap books suit babies who have started to reach for things. A book your baby reacts to is the right book, whatever the age label says.

## How often and how long should I read?

Short and often beats long and rare. A few minutes every day helps your baby learn the routine. When your baby turns away, fusses or loses interest, stop and try again later. In the first years, reading the same book again and again is more useful than always reading something new.

## What matters most in the first months?

Your voice, your pauses and your pace. Reading slowly gives your baby time to respond with sounds and looks, and answering those sounds is part of the conversation. Rhymes, songs and describing what you see together do the same job as a book.

- Several short sessions a day instead of one long one
- Your own voice rather than a recording, because you react to your baby
- Repetition: the same book many days in a row is fine
- No pressure: stop when attention fades
- Board books, then touch-and-feel and flap books as hands get busier
