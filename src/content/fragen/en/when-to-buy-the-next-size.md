The switch rarely matches the label. What counts is where clothes get tight, not the number on the tag.

## How do I know clothes are too small?

Snaps at the crotch strain or pop open over the diaper. The toes of a footed sleeper press against the end. Sleeves and legs end above the wrist or ankle. Necklines feel tight when you pull them over the head. When two of these show up, the next size is due.

## How fast do babies grow?

Fastest in the first months. On average, babies grow about 25 cm in their first year, and growth slows in the second year. Every baby follows their own curve, so measure every month or two rather than going by age alone.

## Should I buy by size or by season?

In the first year, buy by size, because each size lasts only a few months. From the second year, buying by season makes more sense, since a jacket often fits for a whole winter. Secondhand clothes are a good way to cover sizes your baby wears only briefly.

### Size timeline

| Age | Typical EU sizes | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 months | 56 to 62 | Keep a few pieces in the next size |
| 3 to 6 months | 62 to 68 | Buy per size, check snaps and feet |
| 6 to 12 months | 68 to 80 | Buy per size, add season items |
| 12 to 24 months | 80 to 92 | Buy per season |

## What can I pass on?

Clothes in good condition can go to friends, family or secondhand shops. Check for stains, stretched cuffs and loose snaps first.
