This page covers the basic bath products for a baby: water, a mild soap or body wash, shampoo and an optional moisturizer. The label is the place to check what is actually in the bottle. For scaly patches on the scalp, see [what cradle cap is and how to treat it](/bath-and-care/what-is-cradle-cap-and-how-to-treat-it/).

## Is water alone enough for a baby's first months?

No official source we checked settles whether water alone is enough for the first months, so this page does not claim that it is. The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) advises using soap sparingly, rinsing it off promptly, and washing hair two or three times a week with a mild shampoo or body wash. For general bathing frequency, see [how often to bathe a baby](/bath-and-care/how-often-to-bathe-a-baby/).

## Are fragrance-free baby washes better for skin?

The sources we checked do not prove that fragrance-free washes are better for skin, although the AAP prefers fragrance-free body washes or bar soaps with a few simple ingredients. This page does not promise a result for any product. After a bath, the AAP says a small amount of fragrance-free, hypoallergenic moisturizer can help prevent dryness or eczema.

## Do bottle claims on baby products prove anything?

No official source we checked confirms or rejects specific bottle claims, so read the ingredient label yourself. For shampoo, the AAP advises choosing products without phthalates or parabens, and checking the label to be sure.

## How much baby bath product do you need to buy?

No official source we checked covers how much to buy, so this page does not give a quantity. The guidance points in one direction: use soap sparingly, wash hair two or three times a week, and clean the diaper area thoroughly at each change, which reduces the need for full baths and for extra soap.
