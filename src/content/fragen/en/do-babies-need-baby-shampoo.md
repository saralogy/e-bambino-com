The baby aisle is full of bottles, but a baby's bath kit can be short. The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) keeps it simple: mild products, used sparingly, with the ingredient list as your guide.

## Does a baby need a special baby shampoo?

No. The AAP advises washing a baby's hair two or three times a week with a mild shampoo or body wash, so one gentle product can cover both. What matters is a mild formula, not the word "baby" on the bottle.

## Does a baby need soap at every bath?

No. Soap can dry out a baby's skin, so use it sparingly and rinse it off promptly. For heavily soiled areas, use a mild, neutral-pH soap without additives. Cleaning the diaper area well at every change also means fewer full baths and less soap overall; see [how often to change a diaper](/diapering/how-often-to-change-a-diaper/).

## What should you look for on the label?

Turn the bottle over and read the ingredient list, not the front. For body wash, the AAP suggests fragrance-free body washes or bar soaps with a few simple ingredients. For shampoo, it advises choosing products without phthalates or parabens and checking the label to be sure.

## Does a baby need lotion after the bath?

It is optional. A small amount of fragrance-free, hypoallergenic moisturizer after bathing can help prevent dryness or eczema. If your baby's skin stays dry, red or rough despite gentle care, ask your pediatrician.
