The FAA and AAP recommend that children under 40 pounds fly securely fastened in a certified child restraint. Airline rules for bringing and checking seats vary, so plan before you reach the airport.

## Is a car seat allowed on a plane?
Yes, if the car seat has the required label, which reads "This restraint is certified for use in motor vehicles and aircraft." The FAA does not require a child aged 2 or younger to sit in their own seat with a separate safety belt, but the AAP recommends that each child have their own seat. Read the seat's instruction manual and confirm your child is within its weight and height limits before you fly.

## Which car seats can be used on an airplane?
Most rear-facing, convertible and forward-facing car seats can be used on airplanes, while booster seats and travel vests cannot. The label decides it, so look for the aircraft-approval wording on your specific seat before you pack it.

## Should you check the car seat or carry it on board?
If the car seat is not being used to secure your child during the flight, it can usually be checked at the gate, typically at no extra charge. Car seats are generally not counted as luggage. Airline policies on baby gear change often, so ask whether gate-checking costs extra before you book. If you plan to use the seat on board to secure your child, it is not being gate-checked for that flight.

## Can you take a stroller to the gate?
Strollers are generally not counted as luggage, but airline rules for gate-checking them differ, so ask before you book. If you are choosing a stroller for travel, see [umbrella or full-size stroller](/on-the-go/umbrella-stroller-or-full-size/).
