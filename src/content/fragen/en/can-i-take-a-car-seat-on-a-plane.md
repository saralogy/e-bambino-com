The AAP recommends that your baby fly buckled into their own car seat, and bringing it means you have a familiar seat waiting when you land. A little planning before you book makes the airport part easy.

## How do I know if my car seat is approved for planes?

Look for the label on the seat itself. It must read "This restraint is certified for use in motor vehicles and aircraft." If the label is missing or worn off, check the manual or ask the manufacturer before you rely on the seat in the air.

## Which car seats can be used on an airplane?

Most rear-facing, convertible and forward-facing car seats can be used on planes. Booster seats and travel vests cannot be used on a plane. Before you fly, read the manual and confirm your child is still within the seat's weight and height limits.

## Does my baby need their own seat to use a car seat?

Yes, the car seat needs its own seat on the plane. The FAA allows children under 2 to fly on a parent's lap, but the AAP recommends that every child have their own seat and ride buckled into a car seat. Buying a ticket for your child is the only way to be sure there is a seat for the car seat; if you are not buying one, a flight likely to have empty seats gives you the best chance of an open spot for the car seat.

## Can I check the car seat at the gate instead?

Yes. If you are not using the car seat on board, you can usually check it at the gate, typically at no extra charge, because car seats and strollers are generally not counted as luggage. Airline policies on baby gear change often, so ask your airline before you book whether gate-checking costs anything.

### Before you fly with a car seat

- [ ] Find the label "This restraint is certified for use in motor vehicles and aircraft"
- [ ] Confirm it is not a booster seat or travel vest
- [ ] Check your child is within the seat's weight and height limits
- [ ] Book a seat for your child, or ask about flights with empty seats
- [ ] Ask your airline where the car seat can go and whether gate-checking costs extra
