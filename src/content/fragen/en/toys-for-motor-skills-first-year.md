The first year is not about difficult tasks but about movement. Your baby grasps, turns, pushes, throws and puts things in their mouth, and each of these builds strength, coordination and curiosity.

## Which skills does my baby practice in the first year?

Four areas develop side by side: grasping and letting go, rolling and turning, crawling, and fine motor control of fingers. Anything that invites one of these is useful.

| Skill | What your baby practices | Suitable toys |
|---|---|---|
| Grasping | Holding, letting go, passing between hands | Rattles, grasping rings, fabric toys |
| Rolling and turning | Moving the whole body | Soft balls, toys placed just out of reach |
| Crawling | Shifting weight, moving forward | Rolling balls, tunnels, cushions to climb |
| Fine motor skills | Using single fingers | Stacking cups, board books, shape sorters |

## How do I know a toy is safe?

Choose toys without small parts. A common rule of thumb: anything that fits inside a toilet-paper roll is a choking risk. Check for firmly attached parts, no long cords or ribbons, no sharp edges and battery compartments that need a screwdriver to open. Toys sold in the EU must carry the CE mark. Remove anything that smells strongly of chemicals or is damaged.

## How many toys does a baby need?

Fewer than you think. A handful of toys, swapped every week or two, keeps things interesting. When your baby loses interest in something, put it away for a while instead of buying something new.

- Grasping: easy-to-hold toys without small parts
- Rolling: light, soft objects rather than heavy ones
- Crawling: free floor space helps more than any accessory
- Exploring: different textures and sounds hold attention
- Safety: no small parts, no long cords, CE mark
