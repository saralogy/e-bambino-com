Window cords are a quiet hazard, because strangulation can happen in under a minute and is often silent. The sections below cover the risk, the blind types to consider and what to do with blinds you already have. For other household dangers, see [Dangerous Household Items for Toddlers](/safety/dangerous-household-items-for-toddlers/).

## Can a baby be strangled by a window blind cord?

Yes, CPSC says strangulation in a window cord can happen in less than a minute and is often silent, so nearby adults may not notice. A review of incidents from 2009 to 2021 found that a child died in 48% of them. Cribs and furniture placed near a window can bring a child within reach of a cord, which is why CPSC advises moving them away.

## Which window coverings should I buy for a room with a child?

Buy window coverings labeled as cordless for every room where a child may be present, because CPSC says cordless coverings are the safest choice where young children are present. Look for the word cordless on the label or packaging. Our sources do not compare motorized blinds or retrofit options, so ask the manufacturer about those.

## Are cord cleats or cord stops enough to make corded blinds safe?

Our sources do not confirm that cord cleats or cord stops make corded blinds safe, so do not treat them as a full fix. Ask the manufacturer or check with CPSC before you decide that one is enough.

## What should I do with corded blinds I already own?

CPSC advises keeping cords out of reach, shortening pull cords and moving cribs and furniture away from windows. Do not knot or tie cords together, because that creates a new loop a child could become caught in. In 2011, the Window Covering Safety Council offered free repair kits for corded blinds as part of a CPSC-announced recall; whether that offer still exists in 2026 is not confirmed, so check with the manufacturer.
