Window cords are one of the quietest dangers in a home with a baby. A child can become caught in a cord loop in less than a minute, often without a sound, so an adult in the next room may not notice. Of the 209 strangulations and near-misses among children 8 and under that CPSC reviewed from 2009 to 2021, nearly half (48%) ended in a child's death.

## How does a child get caught in a blind cord?

Usually by reaching a pull cord or a loop from a crib, bed, sofa or other furniture placed near a window. Older window coverings can also have hidden inner cords that a child can pull out into a loop, a hazard CPSC has warned about for decades: most blinds sold before November 2000 have such inner cords, and CPSC says to replace them.

## Which window coverings are safest with a baby?

Cordless ones. CPSC calls cordless window coverings the safest choice where young children are present and advises them for every room a child may be in, not just the nursery. Look for the word "cordless" on the label or packaging when you buy.

## What should I do with corded blinds I already have?

Replace them with cordless coverings as soon as you can, starting with the nursery and the rooms where your child plays. Until then, shorten pull cords, keep all cords well out of reach and move cribs, beds and climbable furniture away from windows. Never knot or tie cords together, because that creates a new loop a child can become caught in.

## Where should the crib go?

Away from any window with a corded covering, so your baby cannot reach a cord from the crib.
