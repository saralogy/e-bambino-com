Both systems work. The question is not which one is better, but which one fits your daily life and budget.

## How do the costs compare?

Disposables cost little per pack but add up over two to three years of diapering. Cloth diapers cost more at the start, and you pay for water, electricity and detergent, but they can be reused for a long time and for a second child. Whether cloth saves money depends on how long and how consistently you use it.

## Which is better for the skin?

Either can be gentle on skin. What matters most is changing often, cleaning gently and letting skin dry. Some babies react to particular brands or materials, so if a rash keeps coming back, try a different product and ask your pediatrician.

## What about the environment?

Cloth diapers create much less waste. Their footprint depends on how you wash and dry them: full loads, moderate temperatures and line drying make the biggest difference.

## Which is easier day to day?

Disposables, almost always: no washing, quick changes and easy on the go. Cloth works well at home if you have a washing machine and space to dry. Many families start with cloth at home and use disposables for outings, travel and nights.

| Criterion | Disposable | Cloth |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | Low | Higher |
| Cost over time | Ongoing | Lower with long use |
| Waste | High | Low |
| Washing | None | Several loads a week |
| On the go | Easy | More planning |
