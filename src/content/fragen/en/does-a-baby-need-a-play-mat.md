Short answer: not for development. A mat is a practical extra when your floor is hard or cold, and worth buying for that reason alone.

## What are the main types?

| Type | Pros | Cons | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foam puzzle mat | Cushioned, easy to wipe, covers large areas | Gaps between tiles, small pieces can come loose | Crawling and first steps |
| Padded play mat | Soft, one piece, easy to clean | Smaller, can slide | Lying, rolling, tummy time |
| Activity gym | Toys to look at and reach for | Babies outgrow it quickly | The first months |
| No mat | Full freedom of movement | Floor must be clean and not too hard | Homes with rugs or soft floors |

## When is a play mat useful?

On hard or cold floors, in apartments where noise travels, and to protect wooden floors. Choose one with a non-slip underside, no loose edges or small pieces, and a surface you can wipe clean. A mat that smells strongly of chemicals should air out before use, or go back.

## What matters more than a mat?

Space and time on the floor. Clear an area where your baby can lie, roll and later crawl without obstacles, and offer short sessions of tummy time while awake and supervised.
