Sore or sensitive nipples in the early weeks are part of getting used to your baby's sucking. Pain that stays through the whole feed, or gets worse instead of better, is a signal to change something and often to get support.

## Is pain during a feed a sign of a problem?
Yes, if it continues beyond the first minute or so of the feed. That kind of pain usually means your baby is not latched well, and it is worth fixing right away rather than nursing through it.

## How do you fix a poor latch?
Break the suction first: slip a clean finger into the corner of your baby's mouth so your baby lets go without pulling on the nipple. Then latch your baby on again, so your baby's mouth covers as much of the areola as possible, not just the nipple. If you keep having to re-latch, ask a lactation consultant to watch a feed.

## How long does early nipple soreness last?
Early tenderness should ease as you and your baby find your rhythm. If pain does not improve over the first 1 to 2 weeks, or if the nipple skin is cracked or damaged, talk to a lactation consultant, your doctor or a nurse. Sore or cracked nipples that are not better after 24 hours of treatment are a reason to call your doctor.

## Can breastfeeding pain be a sign of infection?
It can be. Breast pain that comes with a fever should be discussed with your doctor or nurse, and mastitis symptoms that last more than 24 hours or get worse need a doctor's attention.

## When should you call a lactation consultant or doctor?
Get help if any of these apply to you or your baby. If your breasts leak between feeds, [nursing pads](/breastfeeding/reusable-or-disposable-nursing-pads/) keep you dry without trapping moisture against sore skin.

- [ ] Your nipples are cracked or damaged.
- [ ] Sore or cracked nipples are not better after 24 hours of treatment.
- [ ] Pain has not improved over the first 1 to 2 weeks.
- [ ] Breast pain comes with a fever.

- [ ] Your baby has trouble latching or a weak suck.
