Ongoing pain is not a normal part of breastfeeding. This page explains what pain can mean, how to improve a latch, and when to get help.

## Is pain during a feed a sign of a problem?
Yes. Pain that continues beyond the first minute or so of a feed is a sign of an improper latch and should be corrected right away.

## When should you get help for sore or cracked nipples?
Talk to a lactation consultant or doctor if your nipple skin is cracked or damaged, or if pain does not improve over the first 1 to 2 weeks. Call a doctor if sore or cracked nipples are not better after 24 hours of treatment.

## How do you fix a poor latch?
Break the suction by slipping a finger into the corner of your baby's mouth, then latch your baby on again. Try to cover as much of the areola as possible when you latch.

## When should you see a lactation consultant or doctor?
Contact a lactation consultant or doctor if any of these apply. To check how often your baby should feed, see [How often should a newborn breastfeed in 24 hours?](/breastfeeding/how-often-to-breastfeed-newborn/).

- [ ] Pain continues beyond the first minute or so of feeds.
- [ ] Your nipples are cracked or damaged, or sore nipples are not better after 24 hours of treatment.
- [ ] Pain comes with a fever. Talk with a doctor or nurse.
- [ ] Mastitis symptoms last more than 24 hours or get worse.
- [ ] Your baby still seems hungry after most feedings, has trouble latching, or has a weak suck.
- [ ] Your baby is not back to birth weight by about day 10 to 14.
