If you shop across US and EU sites, the size on one label may not tell you the fit on another. This guide explains what US labels mean and what to check before you buy.

## Why do US and EU baby sizes differ?

We did not find an official source that explains why US and EU baby sizes differ, so this page does not give a reason. Each brand publishes its own chart, so check the chart for each item rather than relying on a label alone.

## Should you buy by the age label or by length?

Buy by length when the chart gives it, because a US age label is not a length. US infant garments nine months and under must carry an age label in months, such as "0 to 3 mos." (CPSC). Babies typically gain about 2.5 to 4 cm a month between one and four months, so a 0 to 3 month label can run out on length before weight (AAP).

| What you see | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| "0 to 3 mos." or "9 mos." | Age in months, required on US infant garments nine months and under (CPSC) | Do not read it as a length |
| Letters such as S or M on tight-fitting sleepwear | Not allowed; sizes must be in months or numbers (CPSC) | Look for months or numbers |
| One-piece garment in this range | Cannot exceed 64.8 cm (25.75 in) in length; this is a regulatory limit, not a size chart (CPSC) | Do not use it to pick a size |
| A 0 to 3 month label | Babies often gain about 2.5 to 4 cm a month from one to four months (AAP) | Check length before weight |
