If you shop from US and European stores, the same baby can wear "3-6 months" in one and "62" in the other. The labels measure different things, so your baby's length is the number that connects them.

## Why do US and EU baby sizes differ?

They are built on different measures. US infant clothes are labeled by age; under US sleepwear rules, infant garments sized 9 months and under carry an age label in months, such as "0-3 mos." European baby sizes are usually named after body length in centimeters, but size labeling in Europe is voluntary, so charts still vary between countries and brands.

## Which EU size matches a US 0-3 or 3-6 month size?

It depends on the brand, so go by your baby's length rather than a fixed conversion. Lay your baby on their back, measure from the top of the head to the heel, and pick the size whose length range covers that measurement on each brand's chart. The [newborn clothing size guide](/clothing/newborn-clothing-size/) shows how EU sizes line up with length in the first months.

## Should you buy by the age label or by length?

Buy by length whenever the chart gives it. An age label describes an average baby, while length describes yours. Between one and four months, babies typically grow about 1 to 1.5 inches (2.5 to 4 cm) a month, so a 0-3 month piece can get too short before your baby reaches the age on the label.

## How do you shop across US and EU stores?

Keep your baby's current length in inches and centimeters on your phone and check it against every chart before you order. Size up when your baby sits near the top of a range, because they will soon grow out of it. Check the return policy for cross-border orders before you buy.

| What you see | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| US "0-3 mos." or "9 mos." | An age range in months, the label used on US infant garments sized 9 months and under | Look up the length range on the brand's chart |
| EU 56, 62, 68 | A size named after body length in centimeters | Compare it with your baby's measured length |
| Months or numbers on snug-fitting US sleepwear | Snug-fitting sleepwear may not use letter sizes such as S or M | Buy true to size, so it stays snug |
| A baby at the top of a range | Your baby will outgrow it soon | Choose the next size up |
