## At a glance

- **Topic:** sleep in the first two years
- **Reading time:** about 6 minutes

## How much sleep does a baby need?

Sleep needs vary a lot between babies. These ranges include naps and are a guide, not a target.

| Age | Total sleep per 24 hours |
|-------|---------------|
| 0 to 3 months | 14 to 17 hours |
| 4 to 11 months | 12 to 15 hours |
| 1 to 2 years | 11 to 14 hours |

Newborns sleep in short stretches around the clock. Longer night sleep develops gradually over
the first months.

## A safe sleep space

- **On the back:** put your baby to sleep on their back, for naps and at night
- **Firm and flat:** a firm, flat mattress that fits the crib, with a fitted sheet
- **Nothing loose:** no blankets, pillows, bumpers or soft toys in the first year; use a sleep sack instead
- **Close to you:** share a room, not a bed, for at least the first six months
- **Not too warm:** a room temperature of about 16 to 20 °C, and no hat indoors
- **Smoke-free:** no smoking in the home or car

## Tips for better sleep

### A regular bedtime routine

- **Similar times:** put your baby down at about the same time each evening
- **Calming steps:** for example a bath, a feed, a song or a short book
- **Same order:** the same steps in the same order help your baby know what comes next

### Day and night

- **Daylight:** time outside and daylight help your baby’s body clock develop
- **Night feeds:** keep them calm, dim and quiet, so nights stay different from days
- **Drowsy but awake:** laying your baby down while still awake can help them learn to fall asleep

## Common sleep problems

### Trouble falling asleep

- **Try:** a consistent routine, a calm, dark room, and watching for early tiredness signs such as rubbing eyes or looking away

### Waking at night

- **Try:** keep night-time contact short, dim and boring; waking at night is normal in the first year

### Separation anxiety

- **Try:** short, predictable goodbyes; from the second year a comforter can help

## When should you see a doctor?

- If your baby has pauses in breathing, breathes noisily or snores loudly
- If your baby is unusually drowsy or hard to wake
- If your baby cries for very long periods and cannot be settled
- If you are worried, for any reason
